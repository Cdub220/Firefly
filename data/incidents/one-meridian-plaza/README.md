# One Meridian Plaza · Philadelphia · 23–24 February 1991

A pile of linseed-oil rags in a vacant office on the 22nd floor of a 38-storey office tower flashed over before the first smoke detector tripped at 20:23. Nobody called the fire department until a passer-by and the alarm company did at 20:27; the first engine arrived at 20:31 and saw fire at one window. Minutes later the fire took out the building's only electrical riser room: no lights, no elevators for the next 19 hours. Crews walked up 22 floors, found the stair door locked, and when they got water it came out of the standpipe at 40–45 psi instead of 100 because the pressure-reducing valves had been set wrong. Within an hour the fire had lapped up the outside of the building to 23 and 24; by 02:15 it was on 24, 25 and 26; at 07:00, after almost eleven hours, the commander pulled everyone out for fear of collapse. The fire kept climbing through 27, 28 and 29 and stopped only when it reached the 30th floor, where ten sprinkler heads put out each point of entry. Under control at 15:01 the next afternoon; eight floors gone, three firefighters dead on the 28th floor, 24 injured, ~$100 M lost.
