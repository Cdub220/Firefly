# Incident replay · video script · 90 seconds

Setup before recording: `git checkout main && npm run dev`, open `http://localhost:5173/?view=split&plan=vessel-3x8&demo=1` (recording mode, no controls). Nobody types: keys only. Have `docs/10-incident-replay.md` open in a second tab for the last beat. Speak plainly; the timings are generous.

| time | on screen | say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:12 | Split view, recording mode. Press **2** (freeze the fire's sensor), then **space**. Let it play. | Firefly estimates where a fire is from sensors that lie, and it says how sure it is. Here the sensor in the burning room froze at tick five. The Kalman baseline on the right stays at ninety-seven percent while the fire moves. Ours drops its confidence and flags the sensor. |
| 0:12–0:24 | Press **4** (head to head). Then click **Showdown: blind from t=1**. | Same fire, each brain commanding its own drones. Blind the ignition sensor from the first tick: our world holds the fire to one space; the Kalman world burns to six and loses four drones. The counter at the bottom is the whole argument. |
| 0:24–0:36 | Press **5** (different building). Click the **3D scene** tab; point at the bright stair shaft. | Same system, different structure, nothing retrained. The building is a JSON file; the stairwell is one bright line, and the fire climbs it exactly as the file's rates say. |
| 0:36–1:00 | Press **7** (case file). Wait for the run (about 10 s). Scrub with **→** or the slider to minute 352, then to the end. Point at the "when was each floor known" table. | Then we asked the question a judge should ask: is this useful on a real fire? One Meridian Plaza, Philadelphia, 1991: thirty-eight storeys, eight floors lost, three firefighters killed. We built the building from the federal report, calibrated the physics to its timeline, and replayed it. The white line is the sim; the timeline is the record. Every estimator with sensors knew the fire floor at minute four — the first engine arrived at minute eight. The commander was told about floors twenty-five and twenty-six at minute three-fifty-two; the building's own sensors would have said so two hours earlier. |
| 1:00–1:18 | Scroll to **If that belief had driven the drones**. Point at the ours row, then the Kalman row. | And the result we did not want, first. The first time we ran this closed loop, our honest belief did no better than nobody: with the origin room unsensed and sensors dying floor by floor, our burning set flickered, the allocator re-aimed the hoses every tick, and they never held. The over-confident Kalman was stable, parked the tethers on the right two rooms, and contained it. Same drones, same allocator. We wrote that down, then fixed the allocator, not the estimator: a hose stays on a hot room. Re-run, ours holds it to floor twenty-two too, no drone lost. |
| 1:18–1:30 | Second tab: `docs/10-incident-replay.md`, the Limits paragraph. Back to the app, press **1**. | Limits on the page: a compartment model calibrated to four milestones, symmetric heat transfer, drones are not hose crews, one seed. Everything here is one repository, one command, no network. Firefly. |

Cut points if it runs long: drop beat 3 (0:24–0:36) first, then shorten the limits line to "Limits are on the page. Firefly."
