## 1. Event at a glance

72 hours, six scored moments. Teams come in from schools on both coasts — every 12 hours, judges score your current build against the same rubric they'll use at the final, so consistent progress is worth as much as a strong finish.

- **East:** Harvard · MIT · Columbia · BU · NYU
- **West:** Stanford · Caltech · Berkeley · UCLA · USC

Coast is about where a team was recruited from, not how it's judged — East and West teams compete side by side within the same eight tracks. There's no separate East-only or West-only prize.

## 4. Submission cadence

You'll submit something every 12 hours — six submissions in total, and every one of them is scored the same way.

### Check-ins — hours 12, 24, 36, 48, 60

Post a link to your current build (repo, deployed demo, or working doc — whatever's real at that hour). A judge scores it 1–10 across the same four categories used at the final.

- Each check-in score counts — the five are averaged into one number that becomes half of your placement score.
- Judges score the build in front of them at that hour, not against the finished product it might become — a "9–10" on Innovation at hour 12 means a breakthrough concept, not a finished demo.
- A weak check-in isn't fatal on its own, but five weak ones will cost you more than one bad final demo would.

### Final submission — hour 72

Your finished project: repo/demo link, a short writeup, and whatever you're presenting live or on video. Scored the same way, by the full judging panel for your track.

## 5. What's judged, every round

Four categories, same weights, used at every scored round — checkpoints and final alike.

| Category                       | Weight | Question                                   |
|--------------------------------|--------|--------------------------------------------|
| Innovation & Creativity        | 30%    | How novel and creative is the solution?    |
| Technical Implementation       | 25%    | How well is the solution built?            |
| Business Value & Impact        | 25%    | How valuable is this solution?             |
| Presentation & Communication   | 20%    | How well is the idea communicated?         |

## 6. How a track is won

Your project is only ever compared to other projects in your own track — coast has no bearing on judging.

```
PLACEMENT SCORE = FINAL ROUND SCORE + AVERAGE OF YOUR 5 CHECK-IN SCORES
```

Each of the eight tracks is scored and ranked separately. The highest placement score within your track wins that track's prize.

## 7. FAQ

**Do check-in scores really count toward my final result?**
Yes. Each check-in is scored 1–10 across the same four categories as the final. The average of your five check-in scores is added to your final round score to produce your placement score.

**What happens if I miss a check-in?**
A missed check-in scores as a 0 for that round and will pull your average down — treat every checkpoint as a real deadline, not just the final one.

**Can I switch tracks mid-event?**
No — pick your track at registration and build in it for the full duration.

**Does it matter which coast I'm from?**
No. East and West describe where teams were recruited from, not how they're scored.
