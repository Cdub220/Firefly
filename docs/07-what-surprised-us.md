# 07 · What surprised us

The script for the checkpoint-3 video. Every number is from `results/` (`evidence-cp2.json`, `sweep-latest.json`, `identifiability.txt`, the brief sweep), from `npm run probe:mismatch`, or from the `docs/decisions.md` rows for hours 17 to 27. Five findings, one paragraph each.

## 1. The Kalman baseline's 65 % false certainty is a clean-run number

We expected the textbook filter to be fooled by corruption. It is fooled before any corruption starts. With no failure at all (mode none, demo-6, 60 ticks, 5 seeds) the naive Kalman is confidently wrong on 65 % of ticks, and on 88 % on vessel-3x8; corruption barely moves it (freeze 65 %, blind 72 %, flashover 64 %). The reason is that its burning rule is "temperature above 200 C", and a space that has burned out, or one heated by a burning neighbour, is hot without burning. So the false-certainty headline measures hot-versus-burning separation, which is the thing our estimator does by construction and a temperature filter cannot. Corruption resistance shows up in the other columns: the Kalman's temperature error goes from 2.2 C clean to 42 C under freeze and 123 C under blind, with 33 % coverage of the true fire under blind; ours stays at 0.4, 4.9 and 5.1 C with 93 % coverage and 0 % false certainty on every mode. Blind is the mode that hurts the baseline most: on the full sweep its error is 96 C on demo-6 and its coverage 57 % on tower-5x4.

## 2. Gating does not rescue the baseline

The reviewer's guess was that innovation gating, the practitioner's standard defence, would close the freeze gap. It does not: the gated filter's false certainty is 62 to 69 % against 64 to 72 % naive, and it is a worse thermometer on a clean run (18.7 C error against 2.2), because a chi-squared gate cannot tell model error from sensor error and throws honest readings away. It catches a blinded sensor at once, a frozen one only intermittently, and never doubts the fire the stale value describes. On the full sweep the gated filter is above 90 % false certainty on every vessel-3x8 cell.

## 3. The source-estimating filter fixes the clean run and then loses the fire

The fair fix for finding 1 is the textbook one: estimate each space's heat source in the state and call a space burning when the source is significantly positive. That filter's clean-run false certainty falls from 65 % to 14 % on demo-6 and to 0 % on tower-5x4 (78 % on vessel-3x8, where two spaces have no sensor and a one-space miss in a 24-space set counts as a wrong set at confidence 0.93), and it is the best-calibrated brain on a clean run (Brier 0.028 against ours' 0.043). Under corruption it is as fooled as the naive filter: 59 % under freeze, 73 % under blind. Under flashover its false certainty is 2 %, but only because with its sensors dead its covariance grows and its confidence collapses, while its coverage falls to 24 % and its error to 152 C: it loses the fire rather than lying about it. On the full sweep it is the strongest baseline, and ours has no cell above it on false certainty; it beats ours on wrong dispatch in exactly nine cells, all saturate with a random target, where every hot sensor pins at 300 C and ours keeps burned-out spaces in the set on about half the ticks, at confidence 0.05.

## 4. The estimator did not know that water exists

The closed-loop brief exposed a defect no open-loop sweep could: when two tethers cooled a burning space, the rollout for "this space is burning" predicted a climb toward 900 C, the reading fell toward 300 C, "not burning" fit better, the allocator moved the tethers, the space reheated, and the tethers came back. On clean closed-loop runs the burning set flipped 14, 27 and 36 times in 60 ticks on demo-6, vessel-3x8 and tower-5x4, each tether retargeted A-B-A up to 24 times, and about one drone per run died. The last change before the freeze taught the rollout the world's suppression rule for tethers the brain itself sent: flips 0, 0, 0; deaths 0; and, honestly, the earlier "puts the fire out sooner with a third of the water" was the defect's artefact (tethers abandoning the fire, it reheating, and the crash-cooling on their return pushing it under the ignition floor at tick 29); held on the fire it burns out at tick 34, as under the baselines' beliefs, and ours' wrong-floor count rose from 27 to 32 commands per run. The evidence table, which is open loop, did not move by a byte.

## 5. Physics as a trusted reference survives a wrong plan, and one plan no sensor set can read

The brief asks what happens outside our assumptions. Hand every brain a plan whose edge rates are wrong by up to ±30 % while the world keeps the truth: ours' false certainty stays 0 %, its coverage and temperature error are unchanged to 0.1 C on demo-6 and vessel-3x8 across none, freeze, blind and flashover (freeze on demo-6: 9.0 C exact, 8.9 C mismatched), and wrong dispatch moves by one point. ±50 % is a held-out family. And the identifiability result: on a seven-space plan whose two wings are mirror images with sensors only on the passage between them, a fire in either wing produces the same reading at every sensor, exactly, so no estimator can tell them apart; ours reports both wings as ambiguous at confidence 0.14 with 0 % false certainty, the naive Kalman names neither wing at confidence 0.85 and then the passage, and closed loop the ambiguity costs 104 drone-ticks parked in the empty wing. One sensor in a wing resolves it; one corruption of that sensor takes the resolution away.

## Answers to the checkpoint's questions

Which failure hurt the baseline most: blind (naive Kalman 96 to 123 C temperature error, coverage 33 to 66 %). Which hurt ours most: flashover and mixed, where whole spaces go dark: coverage 82 to 86 % against 92 to 98 % elsewhere, temperature error 48 to 72 C, and false certainty on P(burning) at 0.9 of 10 to 15 % (a burned-out space next to a live fire held above 0.9 for a few ticks); the scalar false certainty is 0 % everywhere. Was there a target where coverage dropped: yes, random (74 to 78 % against 93 to 98 % for ignition, neighbour and far): a named target breaks one sensor, a random target under saturate and flashover breaks every hot one, and under freeze and blind it can hit the space that ignites next; sensor redundancy where the fire will go matters more than where it started. Did k = 3 break anything: no; with a named target there is one sensor to break so the k rows are identical by construction, and with a random target ours' coverage is 78/78/79 % on demo-6 and 74/74/74 % on vessel-3x8 for k = 1/2/3. Did the multi-level plan behave differently: tower-5x4's shaft carries the fire upward fast enough that ours recovers immediately (time to recovery 0 on every mode) and its false certainty on P(burning) is the lowest of the three plans (5 to 10 %), while the source filter's false certainty on it is 0 % on every mode because the shaft keeps every burning space generating; vessel-3x8, the biggest plan, is where the baselines are worst (naive 78 to 98 % false certainty) and where ours' coverage is lowest under flashover (82 %).
